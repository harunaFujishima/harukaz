WHO THIS IS FOR

You've Tasted Matcha Everywhere.
But No One Has Shown You What It Actually Is.

The Problem

"I order matcha lattes almost every day — but if someone asked me to explain what matcha is, where it comes from, or why it's prepared the way it is, I couldn't answer."

→

What This Session Does

The first 15 minutes cover the history and philosophy of Chado (茶道 — the Way of Tea). Where the tea comes from. Why each tool exists. What the preparation is actually asking of you. You leave with real understanding, not just a pleasant memory.

The Problem

"I want to make matcha properly at home. But I don't know which tools to buy, how much powder to use, what temperature the water should be — and every YouTube tutorial skips something."

→

What This Session Does

We bring every tool. You hold the chawan, use the chasen, measure the matcha — guided at every step. By the end of the session, you've made your own bowl by hand and know exactly how to do it again. We send you home with a portion of the matcha and a printed brewing guide.

The Problem

"I've been to wellness workshops before. They feel staged — like I'm watching someone perform. I want something that actually teaches me, at my own pace, without an audience."

→

What This Session Does

This happens in your home, with just you (and whoever you'd like to invite). No other guests. No stage. The pace is yours. You ask questions when you want. You take your time with the bowl. It is entirely private — and entirely at your level.

YOUR 60 MINUTES

The Session, Minute by Minute

0 min
Context
The history and philosophy of Chado. Why this tea asks something of the person preparing it.
15 min
Meet the Tools
Introduction to the chawan, chasen, and natsume. You hold each one before anything is used.
25 min
Demonstration
A full bowl prepared before you — narrated in real time. You observe before you participate.
40 min
Your Turn
Same tools. Same steps. You prepare your own bowl with guidance at every stage.
55 min
Tasting & Reflection
Drink your bowl in silence. What do you notice — about the tea, about yourself?
